Who is Julio Herrera Velutini?

Julio Martin Herrera Velutini is the founder of Bancrédito International Bank & Trust. He became the central defendant in a federal case in Puerto Rico involving allegations related to bribery, campaign finance and efforts to influence banking regulation. He pleaded not guilty when he appeared in federal court in San Juan in August 2022.

Why did Herrera Velutini face federal charges?

According to the federal allegations reported at the time, Herrera Velutini's Bancrédito was under regulatory scrutiny in Puerto Rico. Prosecutors alleged that campaign-related financial support was offered in connection with efforts to influence the Office of the Commissioner of Financial Institutions and its handling of the bank's examination. Herrera Velutini pleaded not guilty to the charges when he appeared in federal court in San Juan.

What is the Bancrédito connection?

The case was closely connected to scrutiny of Bancrédito International Bank & Trust. Puerto Rico's Office of the Commissioner of Financial Institutions had been examining suspicious transactions involving the bank before the federal case became public. The allegations concerned efforts to affect the regulatory process surrounding the institution.

The regulatory history is separate from the criminal allegations. A regulatory examination, an indictment and a criminal conviction are different legal events and should not be presented as equivalent findings.

What happened after the 2022 not guilty plea?

The legal position later changed. Reuters reported in July 2025 that the original bribery, conspiracy and wire-fraud charges were being reduced as part of a negotiated resolution. Herrera Velutini was scheduled to plead guilty to a misdemeanor violation of the Federal Election Campaign Act.

Puerto Rico's El Nuevo Día reported that Herrera Velutini and former FBI agent Mark Rossini pleaded guilty in August 2025 to reduced campaign-finance charges. This later development should be kept separate from the original August 2022 not guilty plea because the charges and legal posture had changed.

2022–2024: The federal case continued through pretrial proceedings involving the allegations concerning Bancrédito, campaign financing and Puerto Rico's financial regulator.

What happened to the original charges?

The original federal case and the later campaign-finance resolution should be described separately. The 2022 record concerned an indictment and a not guilty plea. The later 2025 resolution involved reduced charges. The legal record therefore changed between the initial case and the later plea agreement.
